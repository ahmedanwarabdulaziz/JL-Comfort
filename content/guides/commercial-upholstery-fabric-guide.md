Fabric for a restaurant booth, a hotel lobby or a clinic waiting room has a tougher job than anything at home. It has to survive constant use, frequent cleaning and, often, a building's fire-safety requirements. Here's how to choose contract-grade fabric.

## Durability

Commercial seating is used all day, every day. As a guide, using the **Wyzenbeek double rub** test common in North America:

| Setting | Minimum double rubs |
|---|---|
| Offices, meeting rooms, hotel rooms | 30,000 |
| Restaurants, cafés, bars | 50,000 to 100,000+ |
| Waiting rooms, lobbies, healthcare, transit | 100,000+ |

Higher is better where people slide in and out, such as booths and banquettes. See [double rubs explained](/guides/double-rubs-explained). Our [heavy-duty collection](/fabrics/heavy-duty) is all rated over 100,000.

## Cleanability

Commercial fabric is cleaned often and sometimes disinfected daily.

- **Restaurants and cafés:** a cleaning code of **W** or **W/S**, a stain-resistant performance finish, and ideally **bleach cleanable**.
- **Healthcare, dental and senior living:** fabrics designed to withstand frequent disinfecting, usually **bleach cleanable** and resistant to bacteria and moisture. [Shop healthcare fabric](/fabrics/healthcare).
- **Offices:** a W or W/S code is usually enough.

See [upholstery cleaning codes](/guides/upholstery-cleaning-codes) and [bleach-cleanable fabric](/fabrics/bleach-cleanable).

## Fire safety

Many commercial and public buildings have flammability requirements for furniture and drapery. Requirements depend on the province, the municipality and the type of building, so **always confirm what your project needs with your designer, architect or local fire authority** before ordering.

Fabric spec sheets list the tests a fabric has passed. Some you'll see in our catalog:

- **CAN/ULC-S109:** the Canadian flame test for textiles, often required for **drapery** in commercial and public buildings. [Shop fire-rated fabric](/fabrics/fire-rated).
- **NFPA 701:** the equivalent US flame test for drapery and curtains.
- **CA 117-E (California Technical Bulletin 117-2013):** a smoulder-resistance test for upholstered furniture fabric.
- **UFAC Class 1:** an upholstered furniture industry classification for cigarette ignition resistance.
- **NFPA 260:** a test for cigarette ignition resistance of upholstered furniture components.

## Other things to consider

- **Seam strength and construction.** A durable fabric still needs good upholstery. Tell your upholsterer how the furniture will be used.
- **Pattern and colour.** Mid-tones, heathered textures and small patterns hide wear and stains between cleanings.
- **Fading.** For seating near windows or on patios, choose fade-resistant or solution-dyed fabric, or an [outdoor fabric](/fabrics/outdoor).
- **Ordering enough.** Order the whole project at once so it comes from one dye lot, plus a little extra for repairs.

## Common questions

### What is contract-grade upholstery fabric?

Fabric made and tested for commercial use: typically high double-rub ratings, easy cleaning and flammability testing. Contract fabrics also work well in busy homes.

### What fabric is best for restaurant booths?

A tightly woven performance fabric, faux leather or vinyl rated over 100,000 double rubs, with a W or bleach-cleanable cleaning code.

### Do I need fire-rated fabric for my business?

It depends on your province, municipality and building type. Check with your designer, architect or local fire authority, then choose a fabric that has passed the required tests.
