Pets and nice furniture can live together. The trick is choosing a fabric that shrugs off claws, sheds fur easily and cleans up after the occasional accident. Here's what to look for, and what to avoid.

## What makes a fabric pet-friendly

### A tight, flat weave

Claws catch on loops and loose threads. A **tightly woven fabric with a smooth, flat surface** gives claws nothing to grab, and fur brushes off rather than working its way in. Hold a sample up to the light: the less you can see through it, the tighter the weave.

### High durability

Pets jump on, scratch at and sleep on the same spots every day. Look for at least **30,000 double rubs**, and more for big dogs. See [double rubs explained](/guides/double-rubs-explained).

### Easy cleaning

Muddy paws and accidents happen. A fabric with cleaning code **W** or **W/S**, or one that's **bleach cleanable**, is far easier to live with than code S or X. See [upholstery cleaning codes](/guides/upholstery-cleaning-codes).

### Stain and odour resistance

Performance fabrics resist spills, and some, like **Crypton**, also resist odours and moisture soaking into the cushion. That matters with accidents. See [Crypton vs. performance fabric](/guides/crypton-vs-performance-fabric).

## The best fabric types for pets

- **Microfibre and microsuede.** Very tightly woven synthetic fibres that resist claws, release fur easily and wipe clean. A long-time favourite for homes with dogs. [Shop microsuede](/fabrics/microsuede).
- **Performance and Crypton fabrics.** Engineered to resist stains, and often moisture and odours too. [Shop performance fabric](/fabrics/performance) or [Crypton](/fabrics/crypton).
- **Tightly woven synthetics.** Polyester and olefin weaves with a smooth surface hold up well and are easy to clean.
- **Canvas and denim-style weaves.** Sturdy and casual, good for slipcovers you can remove. [Shop canvas and denim](/fabrics/canvas-denim).
- **Leather and faux leather.** Fur and dirt wipe straight off, though claws can scratch the surface.

## Fabrics to avoid with pets

- **Bouclé and other looped fabrics.** The loops are irresistible to cats and easy for claws to pull.
- **Loose, open weaves and slubby linens.** Claws snag the threads.
- **Silk and delicate fabrics.** They mark, stain and tear easily.
- **Chenille with loose pile.** Soft and cozy, but the pile can be pulled by claws. A tightly woven performance chenille is a better bet.
- **Velvet, with a caveat.** Short, dense velvet has no loops for claws to catch and many people find it works well with cats, but it shows fur and can mark. Choose a performance velvet if you want one.

## Choosing a colour

Pick a colour close to your pet's coat. Mid-tones and **heathered or textured** fabrics hide fur and everyday dirt far better than solid black or bright white. Small patterns also disguise wear between cleanings.

## Living with it

- Keep a lint roller or rubber pet brush nearby for quick fur removal.
- Use a washable throw on your pet's favourite spot.
- Blot accidents straight away: don't rub, and follow the cleaning code.
- Vacuum weekly with an upholstery attachment so grit doesn't wear the fibres.
- Order [free samples](/sample-books) and test them: drag a key across the surface and see if it snags, and see how easily fur brushes off.

## Common questions

### What is the most scratch-resistant upholstery fabric?

Tightly woven microfibre and microsuede, along with dense performance weaves, resist claws best because they have no loops or loose threads to catch.

### Is velvet good for cats?

Often, yes. Velvet has a short, dense pile with no loops, so claws don't catch the way they do on bouclé or loose weaves. It does show fur, so choose a colour close to your cat's coat.

### Which fabric is easiest to clean with dogs?

Fabrics with cleaning code W or W/S, bleach-cleanable fabrics and Crypton or other performance fabrics are the easiest to keep clean after muddy paws and accidents.
