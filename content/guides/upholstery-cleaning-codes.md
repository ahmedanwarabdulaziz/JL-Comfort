Every upholstery fabric has a cleaning code: a letter that tells you what's safe to clean it with. Using the wrong cleaner can leave a water ring, fade the colour or damage the pile, so it's worth knowing before you buy, and before you reach for the spray bottle.

## The four codes at a glance

| Code | Means | Clean with |
|---|---|---|
| **W** | Water | Water-based upholstery cleaner or mild soap and water |
| **S** | Solvent | Water-free (solvent) cleaner or dry-cleaning solvent only |
| **W/S** or **SW** | Water or solvent | Either a water-based or a solvent-based cleaner |
| **X** | Vacuum only | Vacuum or brush. No liquid cleaners at all |

You'll find the code under **Cleaning** in the specifications on every fabric page in our shop.

## Code W: water-based cleaners

The easiest code to live with. Spot clean with a water-based upholstery cleaner, or a little mild dish soap in water.

1. Blot up as much of the spill as you can with a clean white cloth.
2. Dampen another cloth with the cleaner. Work from the outside of the stain inward so it doesn't spread.
3. Blot, don't rub. Rinse with a cloth dampened in plain water.
4. Let it dry fully, then brush the pile back into place if needed.

## Code S: solvent-based cleaners only

Water can leave rings or shrink these fabrics, so use only a **water-free solvent cleaner** (sold as dry-cleaning or solvent upholstery cleaner) in a well-ventilated room. Test it first, use as little as possible, and blot. For large stains or an overall clean, call a professional upholstery cleaner.

## Code W/S: either one

You can use a water-based or a solvent-based cleaner. Start with the gentlest option, mild soap and water, and move to a solvent cleaner only if needed.

## Code X: vacuum only

Don't use any liquid, not even water. Vacuum with a soft upholstery brush and leave anything more to a professional. X fabrics are best kept for pieces that won't see spills.

## Bleach-cleanable fabrics

Some fabrics can also be cleaned with a **diluted bleach solution**, usually around one part bleach to ten parts water, though you should always follow the ratio on the fabric's spec sheet. These are often solution-dyed or performance fabrics, and they're ideal for kids' rooms, dining chairs, healthcare and commercial seating. [Shop bleach-cleanable fabric](/fabrics/bleach-cleanable).

> Rinse with clean water after bleach cleaning and let the fabric dry fully. Never mix bleach with other cleaners.

## Rules for any fabric

- **Test first** on a hidden spot, such as the back or underside of the cushion.
- **Blot, don't rub.** Rubbing pushes stains deeper and roughs up the surface.
- **Act quickly.** Fresh spills are far easier to remove.
- **Don't soak.** Too much liquid can reach the cushion and leave a ring as it dries.
- **Clean the whole panel** if a spot clean leaves a visible edge.
- **Don't wash cushion covers** in a machine unless the fabric says it's machine washable; it can shrink.
- **Vacuum weekly.** Grit wears fibres down over time.

## Choosing a fabric by its code

If the piece will see spills, children or pets, choose **W**, **W/S** or **bleach cleanable**. Save **S** and **X** fabrics for formal or decorative pieces. Performance fabrics such as [Crypton](/fabrics/crypton) are designed to make cleaning even easier; see [Crypton vs. performance fabric](/guides/crypton-vs-performance-fabric).

## Common questions

### What does W/S mean on upholstery fabric?

It means the fabric can be cleaned with either a water-based cleaner or a solvent-based (water-free) cleaner. Start with the gentlest option.

### Can I use water on a code S fabric?

No. Water can leave rings, shrink or discolour code S fabrics. Use only a solvent-based upholstery cleaner, or call a professional.

### What is the easiest upholstery fabric to clean?

Fabrics with code W or W/S, bleach-cleanable fabrics, and Crypton or other performance fabrics are the easiest to keep clean.
